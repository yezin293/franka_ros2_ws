# Active Perception and Closed-loop Manipulation for Single-Arm, Eye-in-Hand RGB-D Robots (2025-2026)

## Key Question 1: What are 2-3 representative papers per area (title, authors, venue, year, core idea)?

### Takeaway
Active perception in 2025-2026 is dominated by VLM-scored or information-theoretic next-best-view (NBV) selection to resolve occlusion/texture ambiguity before grasping; closed-loop manipulation is dominated by "re-observe → detect failure/mismatch → replan or retry" loops built on top of VLM/VLA planners, with the strongest validated real-robot results (vs. simulation-only claims) found on Franka arms.

### Cited Findings

**Active Perception**
- GraspView: Active Perception Scoring and Best-View Optimization for Robotic Grasping in Cluttered Environments — Shenglin Wang et al. (Peng Cheng Laboratory and collaborators), arXiv preprint, submitted 6 Nov 2025 (arXiv:2511.04199). RGB-only pipeline that renders candidate viewpoints off-screen from an accumulated colored point cloud and uses a vision-language model to score each candidate for visibility/grasp utility, then executes only the top-scored pose; reported to significantly outperform RGB-D and single-view RGB baselines under heavy occlusion, near-field sensing, and with transparent objects. — [arXiv:2511.04199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04199v1), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04199v1)
- Active 6D Pose Estimation for Textureless Objects using Multi-View RGB Frames — Jun Yang, Wenjie Xue, Sahar Ghavidel, Steven L. Waslander (University of Toronto Institute for Aerospace Studies, with Epson Canada), published in the International Journal of Robotics Research, arXiv:2503.03726 (submitted March 2025). Splits 6D pose estimation into a translation step followed by an orientation step, then uses an information-theoretic next-best-view policy to pick the next camera viewpoint that most reduces pose uncertainty for textureless/transparent objects; evaluated on the public ROBI dataset and a self-collected transparent-object dataset, reaching high accuracy with substantially fewer viewpoints than heuristic coverage policies. — [arXiv:2503.03726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03726v1), [project page](https://trailab.github.io/ActiveODPE)
- Semantic-Aware Active Perception for Next-Best-View Grasp Planning — Tae Hyeon Kweon, Soo Jeon, University of Waterloo preprint/thesis (UWSpace record, 2026). Builds a semantic-geometric voxel map that fuses semantic detections across views and steers NBV selection toward regions likely to reveal graspable target surfaces (rather than pure geometric coverage); tested on a Franka Emika Panda in simulation (84% success) and real world (10/10 successful grasps) under heavy occlusion, matching a geometric NBV baseline in the real world without prior knowledge of object location. — [UWSpace record](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/0a994fe1-b395-4395-a1ca-b0f7c20c2605), [author PDF](https://taehyeonkweon.github.io/assets/pdf/snbv.pdf)
- (Adjacent/supporting) RoboRetriever: Single-Camera Robot Object Retrieval via Active and Interactive Perception with Dynamic Scene Graph — Hecheng Wang et al., arXiv:2508.12916, submitted 18 Aug 2025. Uses one wrist-mounted RGB-D camera; a VLM selects 6-DoF camera viewpoints (active perception) and the arm physically nudges objects (interactive perception) to reveal targets, guided by a dynamic hierarchical scene graph; reported to outperform existing methods in real-world retrieval experiments. — [arXiv:2508.12916](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12916)
- (Adjacent/supporting) Pickalo: Leveraging 6D Pose Estimation for Low-Cost Industrial Bin Picking — Tarsi, Mastrogiuseppe, Taliani, Cortinovis, Pattacini (IIT), arXiv:2604.04690 (also an MDPI Robotics version). Uses a wrist-mounted RGB-D camera (Intel RealSense D435i on a UR5e) for active multi-view exploration of a bin, with stereo depth refined by BridgeDepth and fused by SAM-6D pose estimation plus a temporal "Pose Buffer"; reports up to ~600 picks/hour at 96-99% grasp success, explicitly framed as circumventing the occlusion/coverage limits of fixed global sensing. — [arXiv:2604.04690](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04690), [project page](https://mesh-iit.github.io/project-jl2-camozzi/)
- (CoRL 2025, but bimanual — noted as foundational context, not a direct single-arm match) Vision in Action: Learning Active Perception from Human Demonstrations — Haoyu Xiong, Shuran Song, Jeannette Bohg et al., CoRL 2025, arXiv:2506.15666. Learns a "neck" camera-control policy jointly with arm actions via a Diffusion Policy trained from VR teleoperation with a shared operator/robot view; it is a bimanual system, so it does not directly match the single-arm eye-in-hand constraint. — [arXiv:2506.15666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15666), [project page](https://vision-in-action.github.io)

**Closed-loop Manipulation**
- Grasp-Then-Plan with Failure Attribution (GTP-FA): A Closed Two-Stage Framework for Precise and Generalizable Robotic Manipulation — Jiahao Xu et al., arXiv:2606.03385, submitted 2 Jun 2026 (NeurIPS 2026 poster). A closed-loop two-stage framework that attributes an observed failure to either the grasp-selection module or the downstream motion-planning module, so corrective replanning is routed to the right stage instead of blindly retrying everything; evaluated on 8 complex tasks in ManiSkill3 simulation and on 5 real-world tasks using a **Franka Research 3** arm — the same arm class as the target setup. — [arXiv:2606.03385](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03385), [project page](https://sites.google.com/view/gtp-fa/)
- VLCP: Vision Language Control Policy Closed-Loop Code Replanning for Robot Manipulation — arXiv:2608.16978, posted 17 Aug 2026. Keeps a frozen VLM that writes robot-control code as Python functions (no fine-tuning); every K steps it re-observes the scene from multi-view RGB images plus proprioception and rewrites the control function, catching failures such as missed grasps before they compound. Evaluated on a 57-task MuJoCo/RoboVerse sweep: 35.1% pooled success vs. 3.5% for a single-shot (open-loop) variant of the same system, with a reported 27.3% within-episode recovery rate after a failed grasp. This is a **simulation-only** result with no real-robot hardware found in available materials. — [arXiv:2608.16978](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.16978)
- PLanAR: Planning-Language-Grounded Agentic Reasoning for Robot Manipulation — arXiv:2602.01662 (2026). Runs on a Franka Panda; checks whether the expected effect of a grasp action was achieved from post-action observation, and retries the grasp when the expected state is not satisfied. — [arXiv:2602.01662](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.01662)
- GraspCorrect: Robotic Grasp Correction via Vision-Language Model-Guided Feedback — Sungjae Lee, Yeonjoo Hong, Kwang In Kim, arXiv:2503.15035, submitted 19 Mar 2025. A plug-and-play correction module that iteratively generates intermediate visual goals from VLM feedback and converts them into joint-level corrective actions, using grasp-guided prompting and object-aware sampling; benchmarked on RLBench (simulation), reported to significantly improve grasp stability. — [arXiv:2503.15035](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.15035v1)
- (2023, foundational/frequently cited pre-2025 baseline) Learning Reusable Manipulation Strategies — arXiv:2311.03293. On a Franka Panda, detects failed grasps via gripper sensing, returns the arm to a neutral pose, re-runs the vision pipeline, and replans; a simple, well-cited template for the "detect via gripper/vision → reset → re-perceive → retry" loop that many 2025-2026 papers still build on conceptually. — [arXiv:2311.03293](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.03293)

### Inferences
- The field has shifted the "where to look next" decision in active perception from hand-designed geometric utility functions (classical NBV: entropy/coverage) toward VLM-scored or learned viewpoint utility, and from passive multi-view fusion toward deliberate single-shot "move to the one best view" strategies (GraspView, Pickalo) — both are compatible with a single eye-in-hand camera and do not require new sensors.
- The closed-loop manipulation literature is converging on a common architecture: (1) a vision-language planner/policy running at a coarse re-planning frequency, (2) a lower-frequency or event-triggered re-observation step, (3) a success/failure classifier comparing observed vs. expected post-action state, and (4) either a retry, a corrective sub-action, or an escalation. This architecture is largely sensor-count-agnostic and maps cleanly onto a single wrist camera.
- Papers using a real Franka arm (GTP-FA on FR3, Semantic-Aware NBV and Learning Reusable Manipulation Strategies on Panda) are the most directly transferable in terms of kinematics, workspace scale, and gripper assumptions to the target FR3 setup; papers validated only in simulation (VLCP, GraspCorrect/RLBench) carry higher sim-to-real risk.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the full author lists, exact page/paper numbers, or formal peer-review status (several of these are arXiv preprints or NeurIPS/CoRL camera-readies without a verified official proceedings entry) for GTP-FA, VLCP, PLanAR, Pickalo, and the Waterloo NBV paper — treat these as preprint-stage claims.
- No ICRA 2025, IROS 2025, or RSS 2025 paper specifically on "visual-servoing-style grasp error correction" was found in searches; the closest hits (GraspCorrect, PLanAR) are arXiv preprints without a confirmed venue, and the one explicit 2025 "visual servoing for pick-and-place precision" hit (arXiv:2501.14557) was withdrawn by its authors, so it is excluded as a reference.

## Key Question 2: Does each paper have open-source code released? License? Hardware dependency vs. portability?

### Takeaway
None of the 2025-2026 papers surveyed here had a confirmed, located GitHub repository at the time of this research; several arXiv listing pages advertise third-party "Code, Data and Media" aggregator links (alphaXiv, CatalyzeX, DagsHub, Hugging Face, Papers with Code) rather than a verifiable first-party release, so code availability for this whole topic area should currently be assumed "not yet released / not found" unless re-checked directly on each paper's arXiv page.

### Cited Findings
- GraspView (arXiv:2511.04199): no GitHub repository found; the arXiv page lists alphaXiv/CatalyzeX/DagsHub/Gotit.pub links but none resolved to a confirmed first-party repo in search results. Hardware: RGB-only (explicitly no depth sensor required for the active-perception scoring step), making it one of the more portable options since it does not depend on a specific depth-camera model. — [arXiv:2511.04199](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04199v1)
- Active 6D Pose Estimation (arXiv:2503.03726 / trailab.github.io/ActiveODPE): project page is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0, but that license covers the website/media, not code; no code repository is linked, only a dataset (T-ROBI) and evaluation folder on Google Drive. Hardware: RGB-based (no depth sensor dependency); the page does not state whether a real robot or only datasets were used for the active-view evaluation. — [project page](https://trailab.github.io/ActiveODPE)
- Semantic-Aware NBV Grasp Planning (Kweon & Jeon): no code repository found; only the UWSpace thesis/preprint record and an author-hosted PDF were located. Hardware: Franka Emika Panda arm, tested in both simulation and real world. — [UWSpace](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/0a994fe1-b395-4395-a1ca-b0f7c20c2605)
- RoboRetriever (arXiv:2508.12916): no GitHub repository found in search results; arXiv page links third-party aggregators only. Hardware: explicitly one wrist-mounted RGB-D camera, single arm — among the most directly portable hardware configurations to the target setup. — [arXiv:2508.12916](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12916)
- Pickalo (arXiv:2604.04690): no GitHub repository found; project page (mesh-iit.github.io) hosts only videos. Hardware: UR5e arm + wrist-mounted Intel RealSense D435i, parallel-jaw gripper — a different arm (UR5e, not Franka) but a conceptually portable wrist-camera, multi-view-fusion architecture; depends on SAM-6D (requires object CAD models) and BridgeDepth for stereo refinement, which are swappable external dependencies rather than hard robot-specific code. — [arXiv:2604.04690](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04690)
- GTP-FA (arXiv:2606.03385): no confirmed code license or repository found; arXiv listing has third-party aggregator links only, and the project page (sites.google.com/view/gtp-fa) describes demo tasks without a code/license statement. Hardware: real-world evaluation explicitly on a **Franka Research 3** — direct hardware match to the target system. — [arXiv:2606.03385](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03385), [project page](https://sites.google.com/view/gtp-fa/)
- VLCP (arXiv:2608.16978): no GitHub repository found. Hardware: simulation-only (MuJoCo/RoboVerse), so there is no physical hardware dependency at all, but correspondingly no demonstrated real-robot portability either. — [arXiv:2608.16978](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.16978)
- PLanAR (arXiv:2602.01662) and GraspCorrect (arXiv:2503.15035): no code repositories found for either in search results. PLanAR's real hardware is a Franka Panda; GraspCorrect is evaluated only in the RLBench simulator. — [PLanAR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.01662), [GraspCorrect](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.15035v1)
- Learning Reusable Manipulation Strategies (arXiv:2311.03293, 2023 baseline): Franka Panda hardware; code availability was not re-verified in this pass (out of primary 2025-2026 scope), flagged as a gap below.

### Inferences
- Because essentially no first-party code was found for any paper in this scan, any adaptation effort for the FR3 system will almost certainly require **reimplementation from the paper's method description** rather than forking a working repository — this substantially raises the effort estimates in Key Question 4 relative to a world where reference code existed.
- Hardware portability ranks roughly: RGB-only methods (GraspView, Active 6D Pose Estimation) and single-wrist-RGB-D methods (RoboRetriever, Pickalo, Semantic-Aware NBV) are architecturally camera-agnostic and would run on the FR3's eye-in-hand RGB-D camera with modest driver/calibration changes; CAD-dependent components (e.g., Pickalo's SAM-6D stage) add a requirement (object CAD models) that is a bigger practical dependency than the camera model itself.
- Papers validated on Franka hardware (GTP-FA: FR3; Semantic-Aware NBV and the 2023 baseline: Panda) reduce the risk that a reimplementation stalls on arm-specific quirks (payload, reach, gripper jaw width) since the target system is literally the FR3 or its sibling Panda.

### Gaps
- I did not independently verify any paper's GitHub page by visiting alphaXiv/CatalyzeX/Papers-with-Code/Hugging Face directly (only search snippets referencing their existence were seen) — a direct visit to each paper's arXiv abstract page is needed to make a final "code: yes/no" determination before committing engineering time.
- No explicit software license (MIT/BSD/Apache/GPL/CC) was found for any codebase, since no codebase was confirmed located; the CC BY-SA 4.0 license found for the ActiveODPE project page applies to the website/media, not to any method implementation.

## Key Question 3: Which approaches could realistically be adapted to a single FR3 + ROS2 Humble/MoveIt2 + one wrist RGB-D + two trays?

### Takeaway
The most realistically adaptable approaches are the ones that treat active perception / closed-loop correction as a thin decision layer on top of an existing perception-and-motion stack — i.e., "move the camera to a next-best-view, then re-run existing detection" or "re-observe, classify success/failure, then re-trigger the existing pick-and-place primitive" — rather than end-to-end learned VLA policies; both GraspView-style render-and-score NBV and GTP-FA-style failure-attributed retry fit this description and sit directly on top of a MoveIt2 Cartesian-path pipeline.

### Inferences
*(This section is reasoning/judgment built on the cited findings above, not a new citable fact — flagged per research-notes convention.)*
- **Active Perception — most adaptable:** A GraspView/Pickalo-style pipeline (score a small set of candidate wrist poses for visibility/grasp utility using either a lightweight learned scorer or even a simpler geometric/VLM heuristic, move via MoveIt2 Cartesian path to the top-scored pose, re-run the existing classical-CV detector) is the easiest fit because it does not require learning a new control policy — it only inserts a "pick next viewpoint" decision before the existing detect→grasp step. The Semantic-Aware NBV approach (geometric+semantic voxel map) is conceptually appealing but requires maintaining a live voxel/occupancy map and a semantic segmentation stream, which is a heavier lift than the pure render-and-score approach.
- **Active Perception — harder to adapt:** The Active 6D Pose Estimation paper's two-stage translation/rotation pose pipeline with information-theoretic view selection is mathematically heavier (requires implementing an uncertainty model over SE(3) pose hypotheses) and is RGB-only by design, so it would need to be adapted (not merely wrapped) to exploit the FR3's depth stream, or run RGB-only and discard the depth channel — either path is nontrivial engineering.
- **Closed-loop Manipulation — most adaptable:** The GTP-FA-style "attribute failure to grasp-selection vs. motion-planning, then route the correction" loop is a natural extension of a pipeline that already has classical object detection + MoveIt2 planning as two distinguishable stages — the two trays (source/destination) also make "did the object end up in the destination tray?" a cheap, reliable post-action success check. The simple 2023 baseline (gripper-sensed failure → reset → re-perceive → retry) is the lowest-effort closed-loop starting point and is already close to what a "classical pick-and-place pipeline" likely does informally.
- **Closed-loop Manipulation — harder to adapt:** VLCP and GraspCorrect both center on a VLM/VLA rewriting control code or generating visual sub-goals; porting either would mean integrating a VLM into the control loop for the first time (a new dependency, new latency budget, and new failure mode), which is a much bigger lift than extending the existing classical detector with a success/failure classifier.
- The two-tray workspace is a natural fit for closed-loop correction research specifically because it provides an easy, unambiguous ground-truth signal (object moved from tray A and is now visible/absent in tray B) that most of these papers have to approximate with VLM judgment or scene-graph state — this is a concrete simplification the target system has "for free" relative to the papers.

### Gaps
- None of the surveyed papers discuss ROS2 Humble or MoveIt2 integration specifics (motion-planning backend, Cartesian-path interpolation, collision model) since none appear to use that exact stack; this is inference from architecture descriptions, not a verified compatibility claim, and should be validated with a small prototype before committing to a full reimplementation.

## Key Question 4: Estimated implementation difficulty and dev time for a single researcher, starting from a working classical pick-and-place pipeline

### Takeaway
No paper reports third-party reimplementation time, so all estimates below are my own inference from each method's described complexity, not a cited fact; expect single-component "add a next-best-view step" or "add a success/failure re-check" prototypes in the 1-3 week range, and full closed-loop or learned-scoring systems (with a trained VLM-based scorer or voxel-map active perception) in the 1-3 month range for a single researcher.

### Inferences
*(All entries below are estimates/judgment, not findings from the literature — no paper surveyed reports implementation time for a third party.)*
- **GraspView-style render-and-score NBV (Active Perception):** Moderate difficulty. Needs: (a) a way to render/predict candidate views from the current point cloud, (b) a scoring function (could start with a simple heuristic — visible surface area, occlusion ratio — before adding a VLM), (c) a MoveIt2 Cartesian motion to the chosen viewpoint. Estimated **1-3 weeks** for a heuristic-scored version on top of an existing calibrated camera + detector; **+2-4 weeks** if a VLM-based scorer (matching GraspView's actual method) is added, due to prompt engineering and latency tuning.
- **Semantic-Aware NBV voxel-map approach (Active Perception):** Higher difficulty — requires standing up and maintaining a live semantic-geometric voxel/occupancy map fused across multiple wrist-camera observations, plus an information-gain formulation over that map. Estimated **4-8 weeks**, mostly in building and debugging the fused map representation, which is new infrastructure relative to a classical single-shot detection pipeline.
- **Active 6D Pose Estimation NBV (Active Perception):** High difficulty if done faithfully (uncertainty-aware two-stage pose estimation plus information-theoretic view planning); a simplified version that only reuses the "take a second view if confidence is low, pick the view that maximizes predicted disambiguation" idea without replicating the full uncertainty model could land in **3-5 weeks**; full replication is closer to **2-3 months**.
- **Gripper-sensed failure → reset → re-perceive → retry (Closed-loop, 2023 baseline-style):** Low difficulty — likely the smallest increment over an existing classical pipeline, since it mainly requires a gripper-state check (already typically available on FR3's gripper driver) plus a state machine that re-triggers detection on failure. Estimated **3-7 days**.
- **GTP-FA-style failure attribution + routed correction (Closed-loop):** Moderate-to-high difficulty — requires defining distinguishable failure signatures for "bad grasp" vs. "bad plan/execution" (e.g., via post-grasp image diff, force/torque or gripper-width readback, and post-place tray-occupancy check), plus two different recovery branches. Estimated **3-6 weeks** for a rule-based attribution version; longer if a learned classifier replaces hand-written rules.
- **VLCP/GraspCorrect-style VLM-in-the-loop replanning (Closed-loop):** Highest difficulty for this team's stated starting point, since it introduces a new dependency (an LVLM API or local model), new latency/cost considerations, and prompt-engineering iteration that classical-CV-pipeline experience doesn't directly transfer to. Estimated **6-10 weeks** for a minimal viable version (periodic re-observation + VLM-written high-level retry decision, not full code rewriting), substantially more to match the papers' full scope.

### Gaps
- These are first-principles engineering estimates from a researcher with the stated background (working camera calibration, classical-CV detection, MoveIt2 planning, grasp execution); actual time will vary with the researcher's prior exposure to VLM APIs, 3D reconstruction/voxel mapping libraries, and ROS2 debugging overhead, none of which could be quantified from the literature search itself.

## Key Question 5: Where is there room for a genuine new research contribution combining active perception or closed-loop correction with this existing baseline?

### Takeaway
The clearest novelty gap is the combination itself: none of the surveyed 2025-2026 papers couple *deliberate next-best-view active perception* with *post-action closed-loop failure correction* in a single unified loop on a single eye-in-hand arm with ground-truth-cheap bin/tray verification — most papers do one or the other, and the ones that attempt both (VLCP, GTP-FA) do so via a VLM-centric architecture rather than a lightweight layer over an existing classical detector.

### Inferences
*(Novelty assessment — inference built on the gaps observed across the cited findings, not itself a citable external claim.)*
- **Unified "active-perceive, act, re-perceive, correct" loop:** Every closed-loop paper found (GTP-FA, VLCP, PLanAR, GraspCorrect, the 2023 baseline) re-observes from essentially the *same* camera pose it already had (or a VLM-chosen multi-view set), rather than deliberately choosing a *new, information-maximizing* viewpoint for the post-action check. A contribution could be: after a suspected failure, don't just re-look from the current pose — actively plan a next-best-view specifically optimized to disambiguate *which failure mode occurred* (slipped grasp vs. missed grasp vs. wrong object vs. placement miss in tray B), then route to the correction branch GTP-FA-style. This is a genuinely under-explored combination in the papers found.
- **Cheap ground-truth signal from the two-tray setup as a research enabler:** Because the two-tray arrangement gives an unusually clean, low-ambiguity success signal (object count/occupancy per tray, easily extracted from classical CV without a VLM), there's room to build and publish a **method that needs no VLM at all** for failure classification — using the tray-occupancy delta plus a lightweight re-view as ground truth — contrasted against the VLM-centric approaches (VLCP, GraspCorrect) that assume no such structured environment. This could be framed as "how much does structuring the workspace (bins/trays) substitute for expensive learned failure classifiers?" — a question none of the surveyed papers directly ask, since most target general cluttered/unstructured scenes.
- **NBV policy learned/tuned specifically for correction, not just for the first grasp attempt:** All three active-perception papers (GraspView, Active 6D Pose Estimation, Semantic-Aware NBV) optimize viewpoint choice for the *initial* grasp decision. A novel angle is an NBV policy conditioned on a *specific suspected failure type* (e.g., "the object may have slipped behind the gripper fingers" implies a different best next view than "the object may still be in tray A but undetected") — effectively a failure-conditioned active perception module, which is a narrower and more tractable research question than general-purpose NBV, and plausibly publishable as a focused contribution (e.g., at ICRA/IROS workshop or as a systems paper) rather than requiring a full VLA retrain.
- **Benchmarking classical-CV-plus-NBV against VLM-scored NBV on identical hardware:** Since GraspView's core comparison is "VLM-scored viewpoint vs. fixed/heuristic viewpoint," and no paper benchmarks this specifically on a single Franka arm with a two-tray pick-and-place task, there is room for a focused empirical contribution: implement both a cheap heuristic NBV scorer and a VLM-scored one on the same FR3 rig, and report where the expensive VLM scoring actually earns its cost over simple occlusion-ratio heuristics — directly useful to practitioners and not yet answered in the papers found.

### Gaps
- I could not find any 2025-2026 paper that explicitly targets "two-tray" or similarly simplified structured-bin pick-and-place as its evaluation scenario (most target cluttered single-bin or tabletop scenes), so I cannot confirm whether this exact setup has already been used as a benchmark elsewhere — a targeted search of ICRA/IROS 2025-2026 proceedings specifically for "tray-to-tray" or "sorting" pick-and-place benchmarks would help confirm this gap is real rather than an artifact of my search terms.
