# Failure-aware Manipulation & Imitation Learning / Diffusion Policy (2025-2026)

## What are 2-3 representative papers per area (title, authors, venue, year, core idea)?

### Takeaway
Failure-aware manipulation in 2025-2026 is dominated by *runtime monitors* that sit on top of an already-trained generative (diffusion/VLA) policy and decide when to flag uncertainty or trigger recovery, rather than end-to-end tactile re-grasp controllers; imitation learning work is dominated by Diffusion Policy descendants that try to cut the number of real demonstrations needed (via 3D/point-cloud representations, single-RGB conditioning, or synthetic augmentation from one demo).

### Cited Findings
**Failure-aware manipulation:**
- **FAIL-Detect** (Toyota Research Institute; arXiv:2503.08558, submitted Mar 2025) — frames failure detection for generative robot policies (e.g. diffusion policies) as *sequential out-of-distribution detection*, trained only on successful demonstrations (no failure data needed), using conformal prediction for calibrated alarms; evaluated on real Franka Emika Panda arms on bimanual tasks (FoldRedTowel, CleanUpSpill), reported balanced accuracy up to 78% in simulation and 72% on real hardware — [arXiv abstract/HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08558v1), [AlphaXiv summary](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2503.08558v3).
- **FIPER** (TUM; NeurIPS 2025; arXiv:2510.09459) — combines two failure signals: out-of-distribution observations via random network distillation in the policy's embedding space, and a novel "action-chunk entropy" score measuring uncertainty in generated action chunks; an alarm fires only when both exceed thresholds; evaluated on five simulated and real-world environments, reported to predict failures earlier/more accurately than prior methods — [NeurIPS poster page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/119369), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.09459v1).
- **"Uncertainty Comes for Free"** (Columbia; He, Cao, Ciocarlie; arXiv:2503.01876, Feb 2025, v3 Oct 2025) — exploits the denoising/generative process of a diffusion policy itself to compute an uncertainty score at deployment time; the robot requests human help only when the score is high, requiring no human-in-the-loop during training; the same uncertainty signal is reused to pick informative states for fine-tuning data collection — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01876v2).
- **FailSafe: Reasoning and Recovery from Failures in Vision-Language-Action Models** (Lin, Duan, Fang, Fox, Krishna, Tan, Wen; arXiv:2510.01642, Oct 2025; listed for IROS 2026) — auto-generates diverse failure cases paired with executable recovery actions inside simulators that have motion-planning support, then fine-tunes a VLM (LLaVA-OneVision-7B → "FailSafe-VLM") to detect failures and propose recoveries; improves three VLA baselines (Pi-0-FAST, OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT) by up to 22.6% average on ManiSkill tasks — [project page](https://jimntu.github.io/FailSafe).
- **RecoveryChaining** (Vats, Jha, Likhachev, Kroemer, Romeres; arXiv:2410.13979, Oct 2024, v2 Mar 2025 — foundational precursor, heavily cited in 2025 failure-recovery work) — uses hierarchical RL to learn *local* recovery policies that fire when a failure is sensed, and explicitly adds existing nominal/model-based controllers as additional discrete "options" in a hybrid action space, so the learned part only decides when/which recovery or nominal controller to invoke — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.13979).
- (Additional/secondary leads surfaced: **TactileReflex**, arXiv:2605.23568, May 2026 — vision-tactile slip/force reflex controller whose thresholds are derived from sensor noise statistics (no manual tuning, no external force sensor), tested on a single-arm + parallel gripper on a pouring task, 9/10 vs 0/10 baseline; **RESETTLE**, arXiv:2610.12185 — disagreement-triggered retrieval and corrective control.)

**Imitation Learning / Diffusion Policy:**
- **Diffusion Policy: Visuomotor Policy Learning via Action Diffusion** (Chi, Feng, Du, Xu, Cousineau, Burchfiel, Song; RSS 2023 — foundational, frequently cited by all 2025-2026 follow-ups) — formulates visuomotor action prediction as a conditional denoising diffusion process over action sequences, enabling multimodal action distributions and stable training; official real-robot setup (Push-T task) uses a UR5-CB3/UR5e arm, two external Intel RealSense D415 cameras, and a 3Dconnexion SpaceMouse for teleoperated demo collection — [GitHub: real-stanford/diffusion_policy](https://github.com/real-stanford/diffusion_policy).
- **3D Diffusion Policy (DP3)** (Ze, Zhang, Zhang, Hu, Wang, Xu; RSS 2024 — foundational, widely cited through 2025-2026 as the standard "data-efficient, point-cloud" baseline) — replaces 2D image conditioning with a compact 3D point-cloud representation, reported to need far fewer demonstrations than image-based Diffusion Policy while being more robust to viewpoint/background changes; real-world hardware explicitly uses a **Franka arm + Allegro Hand + a single Intel RealSense L515 camera** — [GitHub: YanjieZe/3D-Diffusion-Policy](https://github.com/YanjieZe/3D-Diffusion-Policy).
- **DemoGen: Synthetic Demonstration Generation for Data-Efficient Visuomotor Policy Learning** (Xue, Deng, Chen, Wang, Yuan, Xu, Tsinghua et al.; RSS 2025, also ICLR 2025 workshop; arXiv:2502.16932) — needs only **one human demonstration per task**; reconstructs that demo's 3D point cloud and adapts its action trajectory to new object layouts using TAMP-style reasoning; project page reports generating 2214 synthetic trajectories (~147k obs-action pairs) in ~22 seconds, and 74.6% average real-world success across 8 tasks — [project page](https://demo-generation.github.io), [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2502.16932).
- **DemoDiffusion: One-Shot Human Imitation using Pre-trained Diffusion Policy** (Park, Bharadhwaj, Tulsiani; ICRA 2026; arXiv:2506.20668) — kinematically retargets a single human-hand demonstration into a rough robot end-effector trajectory, then uses a pre-trained *generalist* diffusion policy to refine it into robot-feasible actions, with no task-specific training or paired human-robot data; reports 83.8% average success across 8 real-world tasks — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20668).
- (Additional/secondary leads: **Spatially Conditioned Diffusion Policy (SCDP)**, CoRL 2026, arXiv:2606.14535, Korean-authored (Kim, Kim, Ko, Heo, Kim) — single global RGB camera, no depth/eye-in-hand, uses end-effector trajectory as a spatial attention anchor during denoising; **RL-100**, arXiv:2510.14830 — adds a real-world RL fine-tuning stage with PPO-in-denoising on top of an imitation-pretrained diffusion policy, then distills to a 1-step consistency controller for high-frequency control, 8 real-robot tasks; **ACT / ALOHA**, Zhao et al., RSS 2023 — transformer-based action-chunking behavior cloning, not diffusion-based, but the standard small-data (~50 demos/task) BC baseline, [GitHub: tonyzhaozh/act](https://github.com/tonyzhaozh/act) + [tonyzhaozh/aloha](https://github.com/tonyzhaozh/aloha).)

### Inferences
- The 2025-2026 "failure-aware" literature has shifted from classical tactile/force slip-detection (still present, e.g. TactileReflex) toward *policy-internal* uncertainty signals (diffusion denoising statistics, conformal prediction, action-chunk entropy) that require an existing learned generative policy to monitor — they are not standalone add-ons to a purely classical pipeline without modification.
- The imitation-learning side is converging on reducing real demonstration counts (1 demo + synthetic augmentation, or point-cloud representations that generalize with fewer demos) rather than on scaling up massive multi-robot datasets, which matches the single-lab/single-arm use case well.

### Gaps
- Could not find any paper in either area that uses the *exact* hardware profile requested (single Franka arm, ROS2 Humble + MoveIt2, Franka Hand, one wrist-mounted eye-in-hand RGB-D camera, two trays) — DP3 uses Franka+Allegro with a non-wrist-mounted L515; original Diffusion Policy and SCDP use external (non-eye-in-hand) cameras; most failure-aware papers don't specify camera mounting at all. This absence itself is evidence of a gap (see novelty section below).

## Does each paper have open-source code (GitHub link), license, and what hardware/data requirements does it assume?

### Takeaway
Of the papers surveyed, only the two *foundational* (2023-2024) imitation-learning papers — Diffusion Policy and 3D Diffusion Policy — have confirmed public, MIT-licensed code with documented hardware; almost all 2025-2026 preprints in both failure-aware manipulation and newer diffusion-policy variants currently expose only a project page/paper, with code "to be released" or unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings
- **Diffusion Policy**: Code public at [real-stanford/diffusion_policy](https://github.com/real-stanford/diffusion_policy), **MIT license** ("This repository is released under the MIT license," per README). Real-robot (Push-T) hardware: UR5-CB3/UR5e arm + RTDE interface, 2x Intel RealSense D415, 3Dconnexion SpaceMouse, Millibar manual tool changer, 3D-printed end-effector. Demo count per task not explicitly stated in the README (community reports commonly cite 50-200 demos/task for this family of work).
- **3D Diffusion Policy (DP3)**: Code public at [YanjieZe/3D-Diffusion-Policy](https://github.com/YanjieZe/3D-Diffusion-Policy), **MIT license** (explicit in README). Real-world hardware: Franka arm + Allegro Hand + Intel RealSense **L515** (README explicitly warns that the D435's lower-quality depth/point-cloud "might lead to failure of DP3" — a direct, actionable warning for anyone reusing a D435-class sensor). Compute: ~10GB GPU / ~3hr on an A40 for full DP3; the lighter `simple_dp3.yaml` trains in 1-2hr and runs inference at 25 FPS. Demo count per task not explicitly stated.
- **ACT/ALOHA**: Code public at [tonyzhaozh/act](https://github.com/tonyzhaozh/act) (sim: Transfer Cube, Bimanual Insertion) and the separate [tonyzhaozh/aloha](https://github.com/tonyzhaozh/aloha) repo for real hardware. README example workflow uses **50 episodes** of demonstration data; reported ~90% success on Transfer Cube, ~50% on Insertion sim tasks after tuning (`--temporal_agg` recommended for smoothness). License of these repos was not independently confirmed in this research pass (treat as a gap).
- **FAIL-Detect**: No GitHub link found; only a paper and third-party summary page. License shown (CC BY 4.0) applies to the arXiv text only, not software.
- **FIPER**: No GitHub link surfaced in search results beyond the NeurIPS/arXiv pages.
- **"Uncertainty Comes for Free"**: No repository link found; paper license shown is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 (paper only).
- **FailSafe**: No GitHub link on its project page; only a project page (jimntu.github.io/FailSafe). Experiments are in the ManiSkill simulator; robot/sensor hardware and demonstration/failure-example counts are not stated in the abstract-level content reviewed.
- **RecoveryChaining**: No code link found; no license beyond the arXiv paper license; robot/sensor hardware for the stated sim-to-real transfer is not specified in the reviewed content.
- **DemoGen**: arXiv text states code and datasets "will be open-sourced," but no confirmed live GitHub URL was found — [project page](https://demo-generation.github.io).
- **DemoDiffusion**: No GitHub link found; project page only (demodiffusion.github.io); paper under CC BY 4.0 (paper only, not software).
- **SCDP**: No GitHub/code link found; page shows only generic code-finder tool links (CatalyzeX, HuggingFace, Replicate) that do not confirm an actual release.
- **RL-100**: No GitHub link found; only a project page (lei-kun.github.io/RL-100/).
- **TactileReflex**: No GitHub repository found for this specific paper (a loosely related but distinct Franka event-camera slip-simulation project exists at [github.com/tub-rip/event_slip](https://github.com/tub-rip/event_slip), not the same work).

### Inferences
- For a project with a real deadline, the only *low-risk, code-ready* starting points found are the original Diffusion Policy and DP3 repos (both MIT-licensed, both well-documented) — everything else in the 2025-2026 crop would require re-implementing the method from the paper description alone, which is substantially higher-risk and slower.
- DP3's explicit warning about D435-class depth quality is directly relevant: if the existing eye-in-hand RGB-D camera on the Franka setup is a D435-class sensor rather than an L515-class one, point-cloud-based methods like DP3 may need image-space (2D) conditioning instead, or careful point-cloud denoising/cropping.

### Gaps
- Exact license terms for tonyzhaozh/act and tonyzhaozh/aloha were not verified in this pass.
- Demonstration-count requirements ("how many demos") were not explicitly stated in the primary sources for Diffusion Policy, DP3, FAIL-Detect, FIPER, RecoveryChaining, FailSafe, RL-100, and SCDP — these are notable blanks; only DemoGen (1 human demo + synthetic augmentation) and DemoDiffusion (1 human demo, one-shot) give an explicit number.
- None of the code-confirmed repos (Diffusion Policy, DP3, ACT/ALOHA) document a case using a *single* wrist-mounted eye-in-hand RGB-D camera as the only perception input — all documented setups use either a fixed external camera, two cameras, or a non-wrist-mounted depth camera.

## Which approaches could realistically be adapted to the single Franka FR3 (ROS2 Humble + MoveIt2, Franka Hand, one eye-in-hand RGB-D, two trays, existing classical pipeline)?

### Takeaway
3D Diffusion Policy is the most directly transferable imitation-learning method (same robot family, single-camera point-cloud input, open MIT code); RecoveryChaining's architecture (nominal controllers as explicit options alongside a learned recovery policy) is the most directly transferable failure-aware *idea* because it is designed to wrap around an existing classical controller rather than replace it — which matches this setup's existing working classical pipeline.

### Cited Findings
- DP3's real-world reference hardware is a **Franka arm**, i.e. the same robot family as an FR3, and its camera requirement is a **single** RGB-D sensor (L515) producing a point cloud — structurally compatible with one wrist-mounted RGB-D camera, modulo depth-quality concerns noted above — [GitHub README](https://github.com/YanjieZe/3D-Diffusion-Policy).
- Diffusion Policy's official code assumes a UR5 + RTDE + SpaceMouse teleop pipeline and two external cameras, not Franka/MoveIt2/eye-in-hand; adapting it requires replacing the robot interface layer and re-deriving the real-time control loop and camera config (`real_pusht_image.yaml`) — [GitHub README](https://github.com/real-stanford/diffusion_policy).
- RecoveryChaining's stated design explicitly treats "nominal, model-based controllers" as additional options the learned recovery policy can invoke — a design built to sit on top of an existing classical controller rather than to replace it — [arXiv:2410.13979](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.13979).
- FailSafe's recovery-case generation pipeline relies on simulators "with motion-planning support," i.e., the same conceptual role that an existing MoveIt2-based classical planner could play if ported to generate recovery trajectories — [project page](https://jimntu.github.io/FailSafe).
- FAIL-Detect/FIPER/"Uncertainty Comes for Free" all require an already-deployed generative (diffusion or VLA) policy whose internal denoising/embedding/entropy signal is monitored — they cannot be bolted onto a purely classical (non-learned) pipeline as-is; they only become applicable *after* a diffusion policy has been built for at least part of the task.
- TactileReflex and other tactile-slip methods assume a dedicated tactile sensor (e.g., vision-based tactile sensors like MC-Tac/GelSight/DIGIT) that is not part of the stated hardware (Franka Hand + one eye-in-hand RGB-D, no tactile sensor mentioned) — direct reuse would require adding tactile hardware, which is outside the stated sensor budget.
- ACT/ALOHA is architecturally simpler (transformer BC, not diffusion) and bimanual-oriented by default, but the method itself (action chunking + temporal ensembling) is agnostic to arm count and has been community-adapted to single arms; it is a plausible lower-complexity fallback if diffusion-policy integration proves too slow to stand up.

### Inferences
- The most realistic near-term adaptation path is: (1) stand up DP3 or plain Diffusion Policy on the FR3 for one sub-task (e.g., grasping from a cluttered tray) using the existing eye-in-hand camera, collected via kinesthetic teaching/teleop; (2) only once that learned policy exists, layer a FAIL-Detect/FIPER-style conformal or entropy-based failure monitor on top of it; (3) use the existing classical MoveIt2 pipeline as the RecoveryChaining-style "nominal controller option" for safe reset/re-grasp when the monitor fires. Attempting failure-aware methods first, in isolation, is not viable because they all presuppose a learned generative policy to monitor.
- Methods assuming tactile sensing (TactileReflex, most slip-detection literature) are not realistically adaptable without new hardware procurement, which falls outside the stated sensor constraint.

### Gaps
- No source directly confirms whether a Franka Hand gripper (no built-in tactile array) can supply a signal fine-grained enough for any of the surveyed slip/grasp-failure detectors; this would need to be tested empirically rather than inferred from a paper.
- No source addresses two-tray bin-sorting specifically (sorting successes vs. rejects into separate trays as a recovery action) — this is unexplored in all retrieved papers.

## What is the estimated implementation difficulty and development time for a single researcher building on an existing classical pipeline?

### Takeaway
Within the surveyed set, DP3-style point-cloud diffusion policy for a single pick/place sub-task is roughly a 1-2 month effort for a single researcher who already has low-level motion/gripper control and demo-collection capability; layering a failure-aware monitor (FAIL-Detect/FIPER-style) on top of a working diffusion policy is a comparatively small additional increment (2-4 weeks); methods without public code (DemoGen, DemoDiffusion, RL-100, SCDP, RecoveryChaining, FailSafe, TactileReflex) all carry substantially higher and harder-to-bound effort because they must be reimplemented from the paper text alone.

### Cited Findings
- DP3's own documented compute/training budget is light (~10GB GPU, ~3 hours on an A40 for full DP3; 1-2 hours for the "simple" variant), which bounds the *training* cost once data exists — [GitHub README](https://github.com/YanjieZe/3D-Diffusion-Policy). (Data collection and ROS2/MoveIt2 integration time are not stated by the source and must be estimated separately.)
- ACT's README explicitly flags that training time directly trades off against policy smoothness/success rate ("train for longer" is the stated fix for jerky policies), implying iterative tuning rounds rather than a one-shot training run — [raw README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tonyzhaozh/act/main/README.md).
- DemoGen reports its *synthetic data generation* step itself is extremely fast (2214 trajectories in ~22 seconds) once the one seed demonstration and point-cloud pipeline exist, but the paper states the full code/dataset release is still pending, meaning the point-cloud editing and TAMP-style retargeting logic would need to be reimplemented from the paper alone if used today — [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2502.16932).

### Inferences (effort estimates below are inferred from the cited documentation plus general engineering judgment about ROS2/MoveIt2 integration overhead; they are not stated directly in any source, so they are flagged as inferences rather than cited facts)
- **Diffusion Policy (original, image-based) ported to Franka FR3 + 1 eye-in-hand camera, single sub-task:** ~3-6 weeks — 1-2 weeks demo collection (50-100 kinesthetic/teleop demos), 1-2 days training, 1-2 weeks bridging the UR5/RTDE-oriented control loop to MoveIt2/franka_ros2 Cartesian or joint-velocity control.
- **3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) on the same hardware:** ~4-8 weeks — similar to above plus extra point-cloud preprocessing engineering (table/background cropping, depth-quality handling for a D435-class sensor if that is what's mounted).
- **ACT/ALOHA-style BC single-arm adaptation:** ~2-4 weeks — simpler transformer architecture, well-documented small-dataset (50-episode) workflow, but still needs action-space adaptation from bimanual leader-follower teleop logging to whatever teleop/kinesthetic interface is available on the FR3.
- **FAIL-Detect- or FIPER-style failure monitor layered on top of an already-working diffusion policy:** +2-4 weeks — conformal calibration script against a held-out successful-demo set, plus a runtime monitor hooked into the control loop; low marginal cost once a generative policy exists, since both methods are explicitly designed to need no additional failure-labeled data.
- **RecoveryChaining-style hybrid (classical MoveIt2 controller as a nominal "option" + a small learned local recovery policy):** ~4-8+ weeks — most implementation-heavy of the realistic options because it requires building the failure detector, a recovery-policy training loop (the original work uses hierarchical RL, which is itself nontrivial to stand up on real hardware), and the switching/arbitration logic between learned and classical controllers.
- **DemoGen/DemoDiffusion-style one-shot + synthetic augmentation:** ~2-3+ months and higher risk, since neither has confirmed public code; would require reimplementing point-cloud scene editing/retargeting and, for DemoDiffusion, access to (or training of) a large pretrained "generalist" diffusion policy that may not be readily available.

### Gaps
- No source gives a controlled, apples-to-apples wall-clock engineering-time estimate for any of these methods — all time estimates above are this researcher's inference from stated compute budgets, demo counts, and known ROS2/MoveIt2 integration patterns, not a cited fact from the papers themselves. This should be clearly flagged as an estimate in the final report.

## Where is there room for a genuine NEW research contribution combining this classical baseline with failure-aware recovery or imitation learning?

### Takeaway
The clearest, most literature-supported gap is an architecture that uses the *already-working classical calibrated-perception + MoveIt2 pick-and-place pipeline* as the "nominal controller" / fallback / data-generator layer beneath a learned policy and/or failure monitor — a combination that RecoveryChaining and FailSafe each gesture at conceptually (nominal controllers as options; simulators with motion-planning support for failure-case generation) but that no retrieved paper demonstrates on a single real Franka arm with a classical pipeline that was *independently validated before* the learning work began, nor on a two-tray sort/reject bin-picking task.

### Cited Findings
- RecoveryChaining's stated architecture formally treats existing model-based/nominal controllers as discrete options a learned recovery policy can select, rather than training recovery entirely from scratch — but its own evaluation does not specify the hardware/sensor profile needed to judge direct reuse, and it does not provide public code — [arXiv:2410.13979](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.13979).
- FailSafe generates synthetic failure-recovery training pairs using "simulators with motion-planning support," conceptually analogous to using a classical MoveIt2 planner as an oracle/data generator, but its own implementation is simulator-only (ManiSkill) and VLA-centric, not built around a pre-existing, hardware-validated classical pipeline — [project page](https://jimntu.github.io/FailSafe).
- FAIL-Detect and FIPER both show that failure/uncertainty detection for generative policies can be trained on *successful* demonstrations alone (no failure data needed), which is directly compatible with using the classical pipeline's successful executions as the "normal" distribution a monitor calibrates against — [arXiv:2503.08558](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08558v1), [arXiv:2510.09459](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.09459v1).
- DemoGen and DemoDiffusion both demonstrate that a single human-collected demonstration can be synthetically multiplied into many training trajectories, but both start from a *human* teleoperated demo as the seed, not from a classical planner's own calibrated grasp-and-place solution — [arXiv:2502.16932](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2502.16932), [arXiv:2506.20668](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20668).
- No retrieved paper evaluates a failure-aware or imitation-learning method specifically on a **two-tray sort/reject bin-picking** task, and no retrieved paper documents a setup using only **one wrist-mounted eye-in-hand RGB-D camera** as the sole perception sensor for either a diffusion policy or a failure monitor.

### Inferences
- A genuinely new contribution with strong narrative fit to this exact hardware would be one or more of:
  1. **Classical pipeline as a verified fallback/reset controller** beneath a learned diffusion/BC policy and a FAIL-Detect/FIPER-style failure monitor — i.e., RecoveryChaining's "nominal controller as option" idea, but instantiated with a *real, already-validated* classical ROS2/MoveIt2 pipeline (not a simulated or newly-built nominal controller) and evaluated on sorting across two trays (success tray vs. reject/retry tray). This is a combination no retrieved paper performs.
  2. **Classical pipeline as an automatic demonstration/label generator**: instead of DemoGen/DemoDiffusion's single *human* seed demonstration, use the existing calibrated classical pick-and-place pipeline itself (plus induced perturbations in object pose/clutter) to auto-generate large numbers of labeled RGB-D + action trajectories with no teleoperation at all, then train a diffusion/BC policy from that data and measure whether/when the learned policy matches or exceeds the classical pipeline's generalization to novel object poses or partial occlusion — an empirical question not addressed by any retrieved paper (all of which use a human demo or a simulator as the seed, not a hardware-validated classical planner).
  3. **Classical pipeline as a rigorous same-hardware baseline**: most failure-aware/IL papers compare learned methods against other learned baselines in simulation-heavy evaluations; a head-to-head study of "classical reactive replanning vs. learned failure-aware recovery," run on identical real hardware with identical trays/objects, would fill a gap, since none of the retrieved sources run this comparison on a single real arm.
  4. **Tactile-sensor-free, eye-in-hand-only slip/grasp-failure detection**: all retrieved slip-detection work (TactileReflex, SlipSense, the Mechatronics survey) assumes a dedicated tactile sensor; given the stated hardware has none, a vision-only slip/grasp-quality detector derived from the wrist RGB-D stream alone (e.g., monitoring relative depth/point-cloud shift between gripper fingers and held object during transport) would be a hardware-constrained variant not demonstrated in the retrieved literature.

### Gaps
- This research pass could not confirm whether any 2025-2026 paper specifically targets a "no added tactile sensor, single eye-in-hand RGB-D only" constraint for slip/grasp-failure detection — if such a paper exists, it was not surfaced by the queries run here, which strengthens (but does not prove) the novelty case above.
- Could not verify whether DemoGen's or RecoveryChaining's code has since been released publicly after this research's cutoff of search results (both explicitly stated code was pending/not found) — the report's reader should re-check both project pages before committing significant reimplementation effort.
